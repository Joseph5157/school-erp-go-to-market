# Telangana and Andhra Pradesh: market data

> **Draft — Internal review only.** GTM-RESEARCH-01, accessed 2026-11-10. Official figures come from the Ministry of Education's UDISE+ reports [S01, S02]; source IDs refer to the [source register](../competitors/source-register.md). Every row is labelled **GOV** (official figure), **DER** (our arithmetic on official figures), **TP** (news, unverified) or **HYP** (assumption to test).

## About the data

- **UDISE+ 2025-26** [S01] is the latest report found. Reference year 2025-26, data cut-off 31 March 2026. The report states it is based on data uploaded by schools with an active UDISE+ code, and that the Ministry assumes no liability for errors.
- "Private Unaided Recognized" is the UDISE+ management category closest to our target (private schools). It excludes government-aided and unrecognised schools.
- UDISE+ counts **schools**, not legal owners. One management may run several schools or branches. School counts are therefore not the same as the number of buyers.
- The national booklet does **not** publish private-school counts by enrolment size. See [School segmentation](school-segmentation.md).

## Urban, semi-urban and rural considerations

UDISE+ national booklet tables read for this report do not split private schools by rural/urban location for these states. The points below are **hypotheses** to test, not findings.

| Consideration | Hypothesis | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Connectivity | Internet availability is unlikely to be the main blocker for private schools: 92.1% (TG) and 99.9% (AP) report an internet facility (GOV, S01). Quality and reliability of the connection are not measured | GOV figure + HYP |
| Device access | Computer facilities are widespread in private schools (94.8% TG, 99.2% AP; GOV, S01). Teacher access to devices during class time is unknown | GOV + HYP |
| School size | Average private-school enrolment is smaller in AP (≈308) than in TG (≈374) (DER). Smaller schools may be more price-sensitive | DER + HYP |
| Language | Parent communication in Telugu may matter more outside Hyderabad; one regional vendor advertises Telugu parent updates (VC, S18) | VC + HYP |
| Local vendors | District towns appear to have local software vendors (directory listings, S57); local presence may influence purchase | TP + HYP |
| Support expectations | Vendors stress phone/WhatsApp support and on-site help (S03, S15, S17, S18); in-person support may matter outside cities | VC + HYP |

**Software adoption:** no reliable public figure for the share of TG/AP private schools already using a school ERP was found. **Do not assume** a level of adoption. The presence of many vendors with TG/AP pages suggests that schools are being actively marketed to (HYP).

## Practical challenges (to validate)

- **Budget:** free and low-cost plans exist for small schools (S21, S15). A setup-fee model must justify itself (HYP).
- **Connectivity:** reported internet facilities are high, but reliability is unmeasured. Our product needs a connection and offers draft-then-submit attendance only within the business date (catalogue).
- **Training:** average private schools have about 9.5 (AP) to 16 (TG) teachers (DER). Training scale is modest, but staff turnover is unknown.
- **Migration:** schools moving from registers or another ERP need data cleaning. Our Excel import covers students, guardians and placement only (catalogue).

## Data limitations

- No official TG/AP private-school breakdown by enrolment size, board, rural/urban location or fee level in the sources read
- UDISE+ data is self-reported by schools
- One school management may operate several UDISE+ schools
- Regulatory items come from news reports and need legal confirmation
- No buyer research has been done yet
