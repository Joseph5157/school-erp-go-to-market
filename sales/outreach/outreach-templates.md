# School outreach templates

> **Draft — Internal review only.** GTM-OUTREACH-01. None of these messages is approved. Each one is a starting point that a person adapts for one school and sends by hand, under the [privacy and outreach controls](privacy-and-outreach-controls.md). Placeholders are explained in the [kit README](README.md#placeholders). Do not replace them with invented names or contact details.

## Before using any template

- Check there is a reasonable basis for contacting this school on this channel ([when first contact is appropriate](privacy-and-outreach-controls.md#when-first-contact-is-appropriate)).
- Check the **suppression list** in the private tracker: neither the school nor the contact has opted out, on any channel ([opt-outs and suppression](lead-tracker-guide.md#opt-outs-and-suppression)).
- Decide the contact type (below) and keep the opt-out wording that goes with it.
- Do not add prices, discounts, dates, hosting promises, service levels, credentials, staging links or screenshots.
- Keep product statements to what is written here, the [feature catalogue](../../docs/product/feature-catalogue.md) and the [sales FAQ](../faq/sales-faq.md).

## Contact types and the attempt limit

| Contact type | Examples | Counts towards the attempt limit? | Opt-out wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unsolicited outreach attempt**: the school has not asked to hear from us | Templates 1–2; a cold call or walk-in (3, 4); the second attempt and final follow-up (8) | **Yes** | Written messages carry an explicit opt-out line. Calls and visits use the verbal alternative: offer to stop, and stop if asked |
| **Reply to an enquiry**: the school contacted us first | Template 6 | No, while the school is responding | A light line offering to close the enquiry |
| **Agreed appointment**: the school agreed a call, visit or demo | Templates 3–5 when agreed in advance | No | Invitation includes "let me know if you would rather not go ahead"; in person, accept a "no" at once |
| **Continuing conversation**: the school is engaged and replying | Template 7; answering questions | No, while the school replies | A line inviting them to say if they decide not to go ahead |

**The attempt limit (proposed internal safeguard):** at most **three unanswered outreach attempts per school**, at least **five working days** apart: a first attempt, one second attempt and one [final follow-up](#8-polite-final-follow-up-no-response).

- The count is **per school, across all channels**. A WhatsApp message, an email, a call and a walk-in are each one attempt. **Never restart the count by switching channels or by contacting a different person at the same school.**
- When a reply to an enquiry, an appointment or a continuing conversation goes quiet, our unanswered messages from that point count as attempts, and the same limit applies.
- These limits are proposed internal safeguards for owner review. They are **not** legal permissions or legal requirements; the applicable rules are confirmed separately (see [controls](privacy-and-outreach-controls.md)).

**Second attempt wording** (use for an unanswered first attempt, at least five working days later, on the same channel where possible):

> Namaste [Contact name] garu / Dear [Contact name], I am following up on my message of [Date] about [Product name] for [School name]. If a short demonstration would be useful, I would be glad to arrange one at your convenience. If not, just let me know and I will not contact you again.

**Standard one-line description** (reuse it, do not inflate it):

> [Product name] is a web-based system for a school's academic administration: student records, attendance, assessments and report cards, timetable, a guardian portal and school documents. Each school gets its own installation, set up with the school.

## 1. Initial WhatsApp introduction

**Use only when** the contact gave us this number, a referrer obtained their permission, or the school asked us to message. Never to a number taken from a listing or group.

> Namaste [Contact name] garu,
>
> I am [Your name] from [Company name]. [Referrer name] suggested I get in touch. / Thank you for sharing your number at [event].
>
> We work with private schools on [Product name], a system for student records, daily attendance, report cards and a guardian portal. Each school gets its own installation.
>
> Would a short 10-minute conversation or demonstration be useful for [School name]? If this is not relevant, please let me know and I will not message again.
>
> [Your name], [Company name]

*Notes:* send as one message, with no attachments or links on first contact. Use "garu" or "sir/madam" as fits the relationship. This is an unsolicited outreach attempt (attempt 1). If there is no reply, wait at least five working days before the [second attempt](#contact-types-and-the-attempt-limit), then the [final follow-up](#8-polite-final-follow-up-no-response).

## 2. Short email introduction

**Use for** a school's published office email, or an address the contact gave us.

**Subject:** Student records, attendance and report cards: [Product name] for [School name]

> Dear [Contact name / Principal],
>
> I am [Your name], [Your role] at [Company name]. We provide [Product name] to private schools in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh.
>
> [Product name] brings a school's academic administration together: admissions and student records, class attendance, marks entry, report cards that the office reviews before guardians can see them, the timetable and a guardian portal. Existing student records can be imported from Excel after a validation check. Each school gets its own installation, set up with the school.
>
> If it would be useful, I would be glad to show you a short demonstration using a sample school with fictional data, at a time convenient for you.
>
> If you would prefer not to receive further emails from us, just reply "no thanks" and we will not write again.
>
> Regards,
> [Your name]
> [Your role], [Company name]
> [Your phone] · [Your email]

*Notes:* an unsolicited outreach attempt, so it counts towards the limit and keeps the opt-out line. Plain text, no tracking pixels, no attachments on first contact. Keep under about 150 words.

## 3. Telephone conversation

**Use for** a call to a published school office line, or a contact who asked us to call. Office hours only.

**Opening (to the office)**

> Good morning. My name is [Your name] from [Company name]. We provide school administration software to private schools. May I know who looks after decisions about school software, and when would be a good time to speak with them for two minutes?

**Opening (to the decision-maker)**

> Thank you for taking the call, [Contact name] garu. I will be brief. We provide [Product name], which helps schools manage student records, attendance, report cards and parent access in one system. Is this a good time for two minutes, or shall I call back?

**If they agree, ask two or three questions** (from the [qualification guide](lead-qualification.md)):

- "How does the school manage attendance and report cards today?"
- "What takes the most time for your office at the moment?"
- "Roughly how many students and sections do you have?"

**Close**

> Thank you. Would a short demonstration be useful? It takes about 10 minutes, and we use a sample school with fictional data. I can come to the school or share it online. *(Agree a day, time and who should attend.)*

**If not interested (verbal opt-out)**

> I understand. Thank you for your time. I will not contact the school again.

Then add the school (or the contact, if they speak only for themselves) to the suppression list the same day.

*Counting:* a cold call is an unsolicited outreach attempt. If it does not lead to an agreed next step, it counts as one unanswered attempt, whichever channel comes next. A call the school asked for is an agreed appointment and does not count.

*Do not say:* prices, delivery dates, "works offline", "sends WhatsApp messages automatically", "fully DPDP compliant", or anything about other schools' results. Use the [objection guide](../objection-handling/common-objections.md) if questions come up.

## 4. In-person school visit

**Use when** a visit was agreed, or for a brief walk-in to the office during office hours to ask for an appointment.

**Bring:** a blank [requirements capture sheet](../proposals/school-requirements-capture.md) and a laptop prepared under the [pre-demo checklist](../../demo/demo-checklists/pre-demo-readiness.md). Do **not** bring or show login details, staging addresses or real school data.

**Brochures:** the [brochure](../../brochure/) is a **Draft**. Brochures may be given to a school **only after the owner has formally approved both the content and the print version** (recorded in [`docs/content-status.md`](../../docs/content-status.md)). Until then, do not hand out, leave behind, email or show printed copies of draft collateral to prospective customers.

**Walk-in (no appointment)**: an unsolicited outreach attempt, so it counts towards the limit.

> Good morning. I am [Your name] from [Company name]. We provide school administration software. I do not want to take your time now. Could I request a short appointment with the principal or management at a convenient time? If the school is not interested, that is perfectly fine and I will not visit again.

**Agreed visit, suggested flow (15–20 minutes)**

1. **Introduce (1 minute):** who we are and the standard one-line description.
2. **Listen (5 minutes):** ask about current processes, the most urgent problems and school size ([qualification guide](lead-qualification.md)).
3. **Show (8 minutes):** the [8-minute executive demonstration](../../demo/presenter-scripts/executive-demo-8-minute.md), focused on what they said matters most.
4. **Be clear (2 minutes):** state relevant boundaries, for example no fee collection, online only, staff-sent WhatsApp messages.
5. **Agree next step (2 minutes):** requirements discussion, longer demo, or no further contact. If they do not want to continue, say "Thank you, we will not follow up" and record the opt-out.

**Leave behind:** nothing until a brochure is formally approved (content and print version); after approval, the approved brochure only. Record notes afterwards, outside Git.

## 5. Demo invitation

**Use after** a school has shown interest.

**WhatsApp version**

> Thank you, [Contact name] garu. As discussed, I would like to show you [Product name].
>
> Proposed: [Day], [Date] at [Time], at the school / online. It takes about 10 minutes for the short version, or around 30 minutes for a detailed session.
>
> It would help if the person who manages attendance and report cards could join. We use a sample school with fictional data.
>
> Please let me know if this time suits, or suggest another. If you would rather not go ahead, just tell me and I will not follow up.

**Email version**

**Subject:** Demonstration of [Product name]: [Day], [Date]

> Dear [Contact name],
>
> Thank you for your interest. I would like to propose a demonstration of [Product name] on [Day], [Date] at [Time], [at the school / online].
>
> We can do a short version (about 10 minutes) covering student records, attendance, report-card publication and the guardian portal, or a detailed session (around 30 minutes). Either is subject to scheduling and demo readiness. We use a demonstration school with fictional data.
>
> It would help if the principal and the person who manages attendance and report cards could attend. If there are two or three processes you would most like to see, please let me know beforehand. If you would rather not go ahead, just reply and I will not follow up.
>
> Regards,
> [Your name], [Company name]
> [Your phone] · [Your email]

*Notes:* send only after the school has shown interest; it is part of an agreed appointment or continuing conversation. If the school does not reply, our further messages count as unanswered attempts. Never include a sign-in link, staging address or credentials. For an online demo, our presenter shares their own screen.

## 6. Follow-up after an enquiry

**Use when** a school contacted us (website, referral, phone) and we are replying, or they asked us to follow up.

> Dear [Contact name],
>
> Thank you for your enquiry about [Product name] on [Date].
>
> To understand what would help [School name] most, could you tell me briefly:
> 1. Roughly how many students and sections the school has
> 2. How attendance and report cards are managed today
> 3. The one or two processes you would most like to improve
>
> After that, we can arrange a short demonstration focused on those areas. If a call is easier, please let me know a suitable time. If you have already found what you need, just tell me and I will close your enquiry without further messages.
>
> Regards,
> [Your name], [Company name]
> [Your phone] · [Your email]

## 7. Follow-up after a demonstration

**Use within** two working days of a demo.

> Dear [Contact name],
>
> Thank you for your time on [Date] and for showing us how [School name] works today.
>
> As discussed, the areas most important to you were:
> - [Priority 1]
> - [Priority 2]
> - [Priority 3]
>
> Next step: [a requirements discussion using our requirements sheet / samples of your report-card format (with student details removed) / a detailed session for your office team]. After that we will prepare a written proposal setting out the agreed scope. Pricing and timelines are confirmed in that proposal, not before.
>
> You also asked about [question]. [Factual answer from the sales FAQ, or: "That is not part of the current product; we have noted it as a request to assess separately."]
>
> Please let me know a convenient time for the next step. If the school decides not to go ahead, please let me know and I will not follow up further.
>
> Regards,
> [Your name], [Company name]

*Notes:* part of a continuing conversation. If the school stops replying, further messages count as unanswered attempts, within the same limit. Do not attach screenshots from the demo. Do not restate anything that was not actually shown or that is outside the catalogue.

## 8. Polite final follow-up (no response)

**Use once,** as the third and last unanswered attempt, at least five working days after the previous one, on any channel. After this, close the lead and do not contact the school again on any channel unless they get in touch.

**WhatsApp version**

> Namaste [Contact name] garu, I am following up once on my earlier message about [Product name]. I understand schools are busy. If a short demonstration would be useful later, please message me any time. I will not follow up again. Thank you.

**Email version**

**Subject:** Following up: [Product name] for [School name]

> Dear [Contact name],
>
> I wrote on [Date] about [Product name]. I understand this may not be the right time, so this will be my last message.
>
> If you would like a short demonstration in future, please reply to this email or call me on [Your phone].
>
> Thank you, and best wishes to [School name].
>
> Regards,
> [Your name], [Company name]

## Review checklist for each message before sending

- [ ] Reasonable basis for contact on this channel; school and contact checked against the suppression list
- [ ] Contact type identified; if an unanswered attempt, no more than three per school across all channels, at least five working days apart
- [ ] Placeholders replaced only with approved company details and the school's own details
- [ ] Every product statement matches the catalogue or FAQ
- [ ] No price, discount, date, hosting promise, service level or custom-development promise
- [ ] No credentials, staging URL, screenshot or student data
- [ ] Opt-out wording for this contact type present (or verbal alternative used)
- [ ] No draft collateral attached or handed over
- [ ] Sent by hand, by the named owner
